*By Flower, Blue Earth Community Market*

The Blue Earth Community Market has seen a few homes over the years. What started as a small gathering at Bomgaars in Blue Earth has grown into something much bigger — and its most recent move may be the most significant one yet.

For years, the local farmer's market called Bomgaars home. Vendors set up shop in the parking lot, selling fresh produce, baked goods, and local crafts to neighbors who stopped by on Saturday mornings. It was humble, community-driven, and valued.

**The Move to the Ryno Community Lot**

In 2025, everything changed. Ryan Hagedorn of Hagedorn Corn approached Morgan Myers and Ryne Myers of Ryno Repair with an idea: bring the market to the Ryno Community Lot at 112 S Grove St. The space offered more room, more visibility, and more potential. Ryan helped facilitate the move, and the market found its new home.

That first season at the new lot brought a fresh energy. Returning vendors like Pamcakes, Goemann Produce, and Blue Earth Cooperative Bakery were joined by new faces including Briggs' Barnyard, Cookies, Bars & Jars, Granada Gardens, and The Pour Girls. The market was becoming something more than just a weekend shopping option — it was becoming a destination.

**A Partnership Built on Community**

It was during that 2025 season that two people realized they shared a similar dream.

Heather McElyea-Chunn of Blue Earth Cooperative Bakery and Morgan Myers, who came in as a customer and stayed as a co-organizer, spent much of the season talking — about what the market could be, how to grow it, and what it would take to build something the community could rely on year after year.

"Every Saturday we'd talk about what was working and what wasn't," Heather recalls. "Morgan kept showing up, kept asking questions, kept pushing us to think bigger."

What started as casual conversations grew into a genuine partnership. They spent the off-season turning ideas into action: building a website from scratch, getting serious about social media, recruiting new vendors, beautifying the lot, and creating a clear set of vendor standards so everyone — sellers and shoppers alike — would know what to expect.

**Looking Ahead to 2026 and Beyond**

The vision goes beyond Saturday mornings.

Heather and Morgan see the Ryno Community Lot becoming a true gathering space for Blue Earth — a place that hosts the market, but also events like the Hi-Way Motorfest, the Rock-in Roll-ins music series, and a regular rotation of food trucks. The goal is not just a farmer's market. It's a community anchor.

"We believe this space has so much more potential than just Saturday mornings," Morgan says. "We're just getting started."

The 2026 season kicks off May 2nd and runs every Saturday through October. New vendors, new energy, and the same community commitment that started at Bomgaars and found its full expression at the Ryno Community Lot.
